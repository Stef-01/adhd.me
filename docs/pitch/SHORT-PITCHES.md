## Why three design languages and not one brand

The palettes come from published design research on each audience, not from taste. Every
text/ground pair in all three was checked against WCAG AA before building; three patient-palette
values were darkened because they failed.

### Patients — "contained warmth"
`#F7F1E9` bone · `#2A2320` warm ink · `#9B4A2C` terracotta · `#556A4B` eucalypt

Mental-health environment research prescribes a ratio — roughly 50% warm neutrals, 30%
nature-derived greens and blues, ~5% intentional accent — that produces warmth which still feels
contained. Cool tones slow heart rate; warm neutrals avoid the clinical coldness that reads as
institutional. **Fraunces**, a soft optical serif, on centred compositions with heavy negative
space: the layout is doing anxiety reduction, not decoration. Nothing has a sharp corner.

### Investors — "terminal"
`#0E1116` near-black · `#F2F4F7` paper · `#3DDBB8` signal mint

Dark grounds read as sophistication and focus in VC decks, and the terminal association suits a
technical audience. **Space Grotesk** for oversized numerals, **IBM Plex Mono** for data labels
and the `//` eyebrows. Three colours total — restraint is the signal; more reads amateur.
Numbers are the largest objects on every slide.

### GPs — "clinical"
`#F6F8FA` cool white · `#10243A` navy · `#0E6E78` deep teal

Around 72% of leading healthcare brands lead with blue, because it carries trust, reliability and
competence — what a clinician screens for. Teal adds green's healing association to blue's trust.
**IBM Plex Sans throughout, no serif anywhere**: serif reads editorial and consumer, this
audience wants technical. Slide 3 is a real ruled table with a header row and right-aligned
figures, because a GP reads a rebate schedule faster than a chart.

## Structure differs because the ask differs

**Patients** open on recognition and spend a full slide on *what we never do* — for a health
consumer the trust slide converts better than a feature slide. Compliance binds hardest here:
scheduling language only, no clinical claims, no testimonials.

**Investors** kill the two risks that end healthtech seed rounds — timing and payer — on one
slide, then lead unit economics with 3.1×, the number a practice manager repeats when we are not
in the room.

**GPs** answer time, money, risk and autonomy in that order. Every noun on slide 4 is theirs. The
ask is twenty minutes, not a year.
