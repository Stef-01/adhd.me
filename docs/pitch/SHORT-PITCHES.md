# 90-second pitches — three audiences, three asks

Demo-day / elevator format, built to the Stanford StartX–GSB "Giving a Great Pitch" shape:
one idea per slide, 10–15 seconds of narration each, every slide driving to a single ask.
These are **not** cut-downs of the long decks. The whole point is that the ask differs.

**Every slide carries speaker notes** with a timecode, what to emphasise, where to pause, and
the anticipated objection. Present from Figma's Presenter View to see them.

## Why they are structured differently

**Patients** open on recognition ("you already tried to find someone") and spend a whole slide
on *what we do not do* — no diagnosis, no advice, no data sale, no cut of care. For a health
consumer the trust slide converts better than a feature slide. Compliance rules bind hardest
here: scheduling language only, no clinical claims, no testimonials.

**Investors** follow the four things healthcare VCs actually diligence, in order: market gap,
regulatory unlock, **reimbursement** (the codes already exist — no new payer needed), unit
economics with payback, then defensibility, team, ask. Slide 4 exists specifically to kill
reimbursement risk, which is what kills most healthtech seed rounds.

**GPs** answer the four buyer objections in sequence: time, money, risk, autonomy. Slide 4 is
written so every noun is theirs — your records, your rules, your patient, your booking. The ask
is deliberately small and reversible: twenty minutes, not a year.

   practices onboard. Keep it until that stops being true.
3. Figures: MBS rebates at 1 August 2026; AIHW dispensing 2022–23; prevalence 2–6% adults.
   All cited on-slide. Verify before a room that will know them.
